HOW IT'S DRAWN

three.js renders the scene into a low-res target with no anti-aliasing: an orthographic isometric camera for play, a perspective camera for the SHARE mode. The low sun casts real shadow-mapped shadows; bright pixels feed a half-res blur for the night glow. A post pass darkens pixels where depth jumps (outlines), then snaps every pixel to one of 64 palette colours in OKLab, ordered-dithering between the two nearest so gradients stay smooth.

Spectators are one instanced mesh: legs, torso, arms, head and hair per person, with arms and jumps animated in the vertex shader and a wave that follows the nearest cars.

The race runs in real metres on a 5,281 m loop. Corner speed is v = √(μg/(κ − μc)) with downforce c, acceleration is power/drag limited, braking follows the grip envelope. A car's pace (team, driver, tyre offset, wear, fuel, cold tyres, dirty air) is converted into grip, and grip into corner speeds. Overtakes need a straight, slipstream or DRS.

The pit lane length is solved at load so a stop costs 20.0 s against staying out. Each real stop's loss is measured from pit entry to merge against the same car's clean pace over the bypassed stretch, and logged.
