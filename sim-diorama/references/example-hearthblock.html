How it's drawn

The island is a real 48 × 48 × 24 grid of 1 m blocks, meshed into three.js geometry and seen through an orthographic camera at a 2:1 isometric angle. It renders into a small low-resolution target with no anti-aliasing, and the pixel-size slider sets how small.

A second pass reads the depth buffer and darkens any pixel sitting in front of a depth jump, which draws the outlines. Then every pixel snaps to the nearest of 64 fixed colours, measured in OKLab, so noon, dusk, torchlight and rain all land on the same palette.

Light is stored per block. Sky light pours down from above; torch light starts at 15 and drops by 1 per block. At night a zombie can only rise on open ground whose light is 7 or less. Villagers walk A* paths on the grid (up one block at most, down three), and every arm and leg is a two-bone IK chain: feet follow a gait cycle, hands reach for the block being worked.

Original setting and characters. Raw 3D turns off the low-res pass so you can see the geometry underneath.
